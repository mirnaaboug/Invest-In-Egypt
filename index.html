Why Egypt as a regional base

Three structural advantages make Egypt a practical hub for firms targeting more than one region.

Preferential access across Africa

Egypt is a member of COMESA and a party to the African Continental Free Trade Area, which together reach a market of more than 1.3 billion people as AfCFTA tariff schedules phase in.

A canal that is recovering

Suez Canal revenue rose 23% in fiscal 2025/26 as shipping lines returned. The canal sits beside the SCZone ports and industrial zones built for export manufacturing.

The UK is already invested

UK outward FDI stock in Egypt reached £3.6 billion at the end of 2024, and the UK–Egypt Association Agreement keeps tariff preferences in place for goods trade.

Risks to weigh

Shipping

Port Said to Felixstowe

About 3,250 nautical miles via Gibraltar, roughly 11–12 days at 12 knots. Ships rounding the Cape of Good Hope instead add about 10–14 days on Asia–Europe routes.

Indicative distances and speeds for planning only.

Corridors into Africa

Red Sea and overland

From Sokhna and Safaga, the Red Sea route reaches Djibouti and Mombasa for East African markets. Overland, the Cairo–Cape Town road corridor runs through Sudan, Ethiopia and Kenya to Southern Africa.

New cities and rail

Infrastructure pipeline

Priority projects include the New Administrative Capital, a high-speed rail network linking the Red Sea at Ain Sokhna with the Mediterranean, and expanded dry ports and logistics zones around Cairo.

      Relations
      Foreign policy and trade relations

      Egypt's partnerships

      Egypt's diplomatic position shapes its trade access. A neutral summary of the relationships that matter most to investors.

      United Kingdom

      Moving to a strategic partnership

      Ministers and the UK Ambassador have discussed expanding trade, green finance and export industries through 2026. The UK's Trade Commissioner for Africa visited Egypt in November 2026, highlighting renewable energy, tourism and infrastructure.

      BII · Jun 2026
      Africa

      Bridge into the continent

      Egypt is an African Union member, a founding COMESA member and an AfCFTA state party. It positions the SCZone as a manufacturing base for exporters targeting African markets.

      Arab world and Gulf

      Gulf capital, Arab market access

      Egypt hosts the Arab League headquarters and trades duty-free across GAFTA. Gulf sovereign investors have been among the largest sources of recent FDI, including the 2024 Ras El Hekma development.

      European Union

      Largest trading bloc partner

      The EU–Egypt Association Agreement gives duty-free access for industrial goods. EU–Egypt relations were upgraded to a strategic and comprehensive partnership in 2024.

      Multilateral

      IMF programme and reform

      Egypt's reform programme with the IMF covers exchange-rate flexibility, subsidy reform and a larger role for the private sector. The IMF said in October 2026 that energy subsidy reform has reduced economic vulnerabilities.

      Other partners

      Diversified relationships

      Egypt joined BRICS in 2024 and works with China on the TEDA Suez zone within the SCZone, alongside long-standing ties with the United States, including the QIZ trade arrangement.
